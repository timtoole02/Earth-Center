THE SCIENCE OF THE FALL

Gravity doesn't end
at the surface.

Inside a spherical planet, the layers outside your radius cancel out. Only the mass closer to the center pulls you inward: g = G M(r) / r². At the center, gravity is zero, but your speed is greatest. Your momentum carries you through, and gravity then slows you down.

In a vacuum with no energy losses, you reach the opposite surface and fall back again. Uniform density gives a crossing of about 42 minutes. Our simplified five-shell Earth has denser core layers and a different crossing time, calculated by the same simulation.

Try an experiment

Compare the two Earth models. Then try controlled air: drag removes energy, so you won't reach the opposite surface. The simulated 90 kg capsule has Cd × area = 0.7 m²; air density is held at 1.225 kg/m³ throughout. This is an engineered comparison, not Earth's natural underground atmosphere.

Our impossible engineering

A straight, insulated, supported shaft and capsule are assumed. Earth's rotation, tunnel-wall collisions, pressure, heating, and structural failure are omitted. The transparent tube reveals an illuminated artistic cross-section: rock and mineral seams, hot mostly-solid mantle, liquid outer-core metal, and an illustrative crystalline solid inner core. These are not photographs of Earth’s interior. The shaft uses a fixed visual distance scale and follows simulated travel, with motion streaks at high speeds. Telemetry always uses physical distances and seconds. The layered model uses approximate constant densities normalized to 9.81 m/s² at the surface; it is not the full PREM model.
